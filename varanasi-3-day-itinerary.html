A 3-day Varanasi itinerary at an easy pace

Three days lets you see the big sights without rushing, with afternoons to rest.

This plan spreads our 2-day itinerary over three days and adds a slower morning. Timings change on festival days, so check the evening before.

Day 1: Ghats and the evening aarti

Morning: Assi Ghat

Start with the morning aarti at Assi Ghat (around 5:30 am), then walk the ghats north as the city wakes up.

Afternoon: rest

Come back, eat, and rest through the hottest part of the day.

Evening: Ganga aarti at Dashashwamedh

Around 5:45 pm in winter (Nov–Jan) and 6:45 pm in summer (Apr–Sep). Reach an hour early for a good spot.

Day 2: Kashi Vishwanath and the old city

Early morning: Kashi Vishwanath

General darshan opens at 4 am. See our full timings.

Late morning: old lanes and breakfast

Walk the lanes around the temple and try kachori-sabzi and jalebi, then lassi or malaiyo in season.

Evening: Banarasi silk

Browse Banarasi silk sarees and fabrics, then sit on the ghat steps as the lamps come on.

Day 3: Temples, BHU and Sarnath

Morning: Durga Kund and Sankat Mochan

Two of the city's best-known temples, close to each other. Go early.

Midday: BHU campus

The green Banaras Hindu University campus and the New Vishwanath Temple make a calm break from the old city.

Afternoon: Sarnath

Allow two to three hours plus travel. The museum is closed on Fridays. See our Sarnath half-day guide.

Getting around

  • Cabs work well for longer hops; expect to walk the last stretch near the ghats.

Questions

Is 3 days enough for Varanasi?

Yes. Three days cover the ghats, Kashi Vishwanath, the evening aarti, the main temples and Sarnath at a comfortable pace.

When is the best time to visit Varanasi?

October to March has the most pleasant weather. November to February is the busiest season.

Is Sarnath open every day?

The Sarnath site is open daily, but the Sarnath Museum is closed on Fridays.
