The short answer

For most visitors, October to March is the best time to visit Varanasi. Days are dry and pleasant, evenings are cool enough for long walks along the ghats, and the city's biggest festivals fall in these months. If you want the calmest mix of good weather and manageable crowds, aim for November (outside Dev Deepawali week) or February to early March.

That said, there is no wrong time to come to Kashi. Every season has its own character, and many pilgrims choose the hot or wet months on purpose, for Sawan or a family ritual. The trick is to plan your day around the weather.

October to March: the peak season

October is still warm in the day but the rains have gone. Navratri and Dussehra bring the city alive, and Diwali usually falls in late October or November.

November is the favourite month for many travellers. The air is dry, the sky is clear and evenings at the ghats are comfortable. On Kartik Purnima the ghats are lit with lakhs of diyas for Dev Deepawali, one of the most beautiful nights in India, and also one of the busiest. Rooms around that date fill weeks ahead.

December and January are cool and atmospheric. Nights drop to around 9 to 10°C, and dense morning fog is common, which can delay early trains and flights. Sunrise at the ghats is softer and mistier, and the Christmas to New Year week is very busy. Makar Sankranti in mid-January brings pilgrims for a holy dip in the Ganga.

February and early March are a sweet spot: warm, sunny days and fewer holiday crowds. Maha Shivratri, the great night of Lord Shiva, is the biggest day of the year at the Shiva temples of Kashi (in 2027 it falls on Saturday, 6 March). Holi follows in March, with Rangbhari Ekadashi a few days before it, when the colours begin in Kashi.

April to June: summer

Summer in Varanasi is intense. Daytime highs reach 39 to 41°C and the heat lasts into the evening. The upside is lower prices and quieter streets. If you visit now, start early: do the ghats and temples from first light until about 10 am, rest in an air-conditioned room through the afternoon, and head out again after sunset for the evening aarti. Drink plenty of water and carry a cap or scarf.

July to September: monsoon and Sawan

Most of the year's rain falls in July and August. The Ganga swells, the lower ghats can go under water, and walking from ghat to ghat is often not possible. In high-water weeks, the evening aarti is held on higher ground. Lanes in the old city get muddy, so good sandals help.

This is also the holy month of Sawan (Shravan), when Kanwariyas and devotees arrive in huge numbers, especially on Mondays. If you are coming for Sawan darshan, book early and expect long queues. If you want a quiet trip, pick another season.

Tips for any season

  • Temple order: by tradition, visit Kaal Bhairav, the Kotwal of Kashi, first, and then go for darshan at Kashi Vishwanath. See our Kashi Vishwanath timings guide.
  • Go early: weekday mornings are usually calmer than evenings and weekends, in every season.
  • Check festival timings: temple hours change on Maha Shivratri, Sawan Mondays and other festivals. Check the official temple website the day before.
  • Book ahead for big dates: Dev Deepawali, Maha Shivratri, Holi, Sawan, long weekends and the year-end holidays.
  • Pack for the season: a warm jacket and layers for December and January; light cotton, sunscreen and a water bottle for April to June; an umbrella and quick-dry sandals for the monsoon.

Questions

What is the best month to visit Varanasi?

November, February and early March are the most comfortable: dry, sunny days of about 25 to 30°C and cool evenings. November also has Dev Deepawali, so book early if your dates fall around Kartik Purnima.

Is Varanasi too cold in December and January?

Days are mild at around 24 to 25°C, but nights drop to about 9 to 10°C and early mornings are often foggy. Pack a warm jacket and allow for fog delays on early trains and flights.

Is it a good idea to visit Varanasi in summer?

April to June is very hot, with daytime highs around 39 to 41°C. It is possible if you plan temple visits and ghat walks for early morning and evening and rest indoors in the afternoon.

What is Varanasi like in the monsoon?

July and August are the wettest months. The Ganga rises, the lower ghats can go under water and walking along the riverfront is limited. Sawan brings large crowds of pilgrims, especially on Mondays.

When are the biggest crowds in Varanasi?

Around Maha Shivratri, Holi, Sawan Mondays, Dev Deepawali, long weekends and the Christmas to New Year holidays. Book rooms and darshan tickets well ahead for these dates.

Which temple should I visit first, Kaal Bhairav or Kashi Vishwanath?

By tradition, visit Kaal Bhairav, the Kotwal (guardian) of Kashi, first, and then go for darshan at Kashi Vishwanath.
